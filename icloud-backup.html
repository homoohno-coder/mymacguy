iCloud & Backup
Help

iCloud full, photos not syncing, lost files? Here's how to manage your iCloud storage, recover deleted files, and make sure you never lose anything again.

Fix it yourself

iCloud & Backup Solutions

1

Check what's taking up your iCloud storage

On iPhone: Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. On Mac: System Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Manage. You'll see exactly what's using space — usually it's Photos, iPhone backups, or iCloud Drive. Delete old device backups for phones you no longer own.

2

Recover deleted photos (you have 30 days)

On iPhone: open Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted. On Mac: open Photos → Recently Deleted in the sidebar. Photos stay there for 30 days before permanent deletion. Select what you want and tap Recover.

3

Recover deleted files from iCloud Drive

Go to icloud.com → sign in → click iCloud Drive. Scroll down and click Recently Deleted in the sidebar. Files are kept for 30 days. You can also recover deleted Notes, Contacts, Calendars, and Reminders from the iCloud.com dashboard.

4

Fix photos not syncing to iCloud

Make sure iCloud Photos is enabled: Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Photos → toggle on. Check that you have enough iCloud storage. Also check Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Show All — make sure Photos is set to sync. Sometimes signing out and back into iCloud fixes stubborn sync issues.

5

Set up Time Machine on your Mac (free local backup)

Plug in an external drive (any USB or Thunderbolt drive works). Go to System Settings → General → Time Machine → Add Backup Disk. Time Machine backs up hourly automatically. If your Mac dies or files get accidentally deleted, you can restore everything. A 1TB drive costs ~$50 on Amazon.

6

Back up your iPhone manually right now

To iCloud: Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → iCloud Backup → Back Up Now.
To your Mac: Connect iPhone → open Finder → select your iPhone in the sidebar → click Back Up Now. A local backup is faster and doesn't count against your iCloud storage.

Tips & Hacks

Smart Backup Strategy

💰

iCloud Storage Pricing — What You Actually Need

The free 5 GB fills up fast. Here's Apple's pricing: 50 GB = $0.99/month, 200 GB = $2.99/month (can share with family), 2 TB = $9.99/month. For most people, 50 GB or 200 GB is plenty. The 200 GB plan shared across a family of 4–5 is an incredible deal.

Don't need as much storage as you think? Optimize iPhone Storage (Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Photos → Optimize iPhone Storage) keeps full-resolution photos in iCloud and smaller versions on your phone — without losing anything.

🔄

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

3 copies of your data. 2 different storage types. 1 offsite. For Apple users: iCloud (offsite), Time Machine on an external drive (local), and optionally a second cloud service like Backblaze ($99/year for unlimited Mac backup).

🌥️

Backblaze — Unlimited Mac Backup for $99/year

Backblaze backs up your entire Mac to the cloud continuously for ~$99/year. If your house burns down and you have both Time Machine and Backblaze, you lose nothing. Strongly recommended for anyone with irreplaceable files.

📸

Google Photos — Free Backup Alternative

Google Photos gives 15 GB free and offers very affordable storage. Use it as a second backup for your photos alongside iCloud. The search feature (finds photos by content) is genuinely better than Apple's.

Access All Your iCloud Files from the Web

Everything in iCloud Drive, Photos, Notes, Contacts, and more is accessible at icloud.com from any browser — even on a Windows PC or Android phone. Useful when you're using someone else's computer.

🗑️

Free Up iCloud Space Without Paying More

Delete old iPhone/iPad backups for devices you no longer own: Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud → Manage Storage → Backups. Each old backup can take 2–5 GB. Also delete duplicate photos with a free app like Smart Cleaner.
