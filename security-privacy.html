Privacy Help

Hacked account, suspicious activity, or just want to lock things down? Here's how to protect yourself — step by step, for free.

Fix it yourself

Account Recovery & Security Fixes

1

Check if your email or password was exposed in a breach

Go to haveibeenpwned.com and enter your email address. It's completely free and shows every data breach your email appeared in. If you show up, change that password everywhere you used it.

2

Recover a locked or hacked Apple ID

Go to iforgot.apple.com to reset your Apple ID password. If you've been locked out, follow the account recovery process — Apple will verify your identity and send a reset link to a trusted device or phone number.

3

Enable Two-Factor Authentication on your Apple ID

On your iPhone: Settings → [Your Name] → Sign-In & Security → Two-Factor Authentication. This means even if someone knows your password, they can't get in without a code sent to your phone. Do this today.

4

Scan for malware with Malwarebytes (free)

Download Malwarebytes for Mac — it's free to scan and remove adware, browser hijackers, and malware. Run a scan if your browser has new toolbars, you're seeing popup ads, or your Mac is acting weird.

5

Use iCloud Keychain to manage passwords (it's built in & free)

On Mac: System Settings → Passwords. On iPhone: Settings → Passwords. iCloud Keychain saves, fills, and generates strong passwords automatically. It also flags reused and compromised passwords. No need to pay for a third-party password manager.

6

Enable FileVault to encrypt your Mac's hard drive

Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → FileVault → Turn On. This encrypts your entire disk so if your Mac is stolen, no one can access your files without your password. Takes a few hours to encrypt but runs in the background.

7

Remove suspicious login items and extensions

On Mac: System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions — remove anything you don't recognize. In Safari/Chrome: check Extensions settings and remove unfamiliar ones. Malware often hides here.

Free Tools & Tips

Protect Yourself for Free

🦠

Malwarebytes — Best Free Mac Malware Scanner

Malwarebytes for Mac is the gold standard for removing adware and malware. The free version scans and removes threats. You don't need the paid subscription for basic protection.

🔍

KnockKnock — See What's Running on Your Mac

KnockKnock (free) from Objective-See shows everything that's set to run persistently on your Mac. Great for spotting hidden malware. Also check out their other free tools like LuLu (free firewall).

🌐

Use a VPN on Public Wi-Fi

Never do banking or enter passwords on coffee shop or airport Wi-Fi without a VPN. ProtonVPN has a solid free tier. Or use your iPhone as a personal hotspot instead — far more secure.

🔑

Bitwarden — Free Password Manager

If you want more than iCloud Keychain, Bitwarden is a free, open-source password manager that works on everything. Far better than reusing the same password.

🖥️

Remote Security Help from Wahed

If you think you've been hacked and need help securing your accounts, Wahed can walk you through it remotely via Zoho Assist — click the link and share your screen.

📵

Got a "Your Mac is infected" popup?

These are 100% scams. Do NOT call the number. Do NOT click anything. Press Cmd + Q to quit your browser. If it won't close, hold the power button to force a shutdown. Then run a Malwarebytes scan.
