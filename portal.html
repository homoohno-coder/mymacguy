Quick fixes

Try these before calling.

The five things I'd walk you through first anyway. If the answer's not here, call me.

My Mac is slow or unresponsive — what should I try first?

Restart properly (Apple menu → Restart, not just close the lid). Then check available storage: Apple menu → About This Mac → Storage. If you're below 15% free, that alone explains most slowness. If a restart helped briefly but it came back, time to call.

Wi-Fi is dropping or slow on one device.

Forget and rejoin the network: System Settings → Wi-Fi → click the network → Forget This Network → reconnect with the password. Then power-cycle your modem and router (unplug both, wait 60 seconds, modem first, then router). If only one device misbehaves while others are fine, that device's Wi-Fi settings or driver are likely the issue.

I'm locked out of my Apple ID.

Go to iforgot.apple.com on any device. Apple will walk you through identity verification using one of your trusted devices. If you've lost access to all trusted devices, the recovery process takes several days — call me before starting that, there's often a faster path.

Email stopped working on iPhone or Mac.

Most common cause: a password change that didn't propagate. Open Settings → Mail → Accounts (iOS) or System Settings → Internet Accounts (Mac), tap the account, and re-enter the password. If you use Gmail, Yahoo, or Outlook with two-factor authentication, you need an app-specific password — call me if that's the case.

How do I make sure my data is backed up?

Two things, today: (1) plug in a USB-C external drive at least 2× the size of your Mac's drive and turn on Time Machine (System Settings → General → Time Machine); (2) verify iCloud Drive sync is on for Desktop and Documents (System Settings → Apple ID → iCloud). That's the minimum. The real strategy adds an off-site cloud backup — that's a 60-minute conversation worth having.

Should I update macOS / iOS right away?

Short answer: not on day one. Wait 1–2 weeks for the inevitable .1 release that fixes the things Apple missed. Always back up first (see above). For business use, wait longer — at least one full minor version. If you depend on specific apps, check their compatibility before updating.
