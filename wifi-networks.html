Wi-Fi & Network Troubleshooting

1

Restart your modem and router (the right way)

Unplug your modem and router from power. Wait a full 60 seconds — not 10, not 30. Plug the modem in first, wait 30 seconds until the lights stabilize, then plug in the router. This alone fixes the majority of home internet issues.

2

Check if it's your ISP — not your equipment

Visit downdetector.com or your ISP's outage map. If there's a known outage in your area, no amount of router rebooting will fix it — you just have to wait. Also test: plug directly into your modem with an Ethernet cable. If that works fine, the problem is your router or Wi-Fi, not your internet service.

3

Test your actual internet speed

Go to fast.com (by Netflix) or speedtest.net on a device connected to Wi-Fi, then again plugged into Ethernet. If wired is fast but Wi-Fi is slow, your router or its placement is the issue. If both are slow, call your ISP.

4

Forget and rejoin your Wi-Fi network on Mac

Hold Option and click the Wi-Fi icon → Open Wi-Fi Settings → click the (i) next to your network → Forget This Network. Then reconnect fresh. On iPhone: Settings → Wi-Fi → tap (i) next to network → Forget This Network.

5

Switch to a faster DNS server

Your ISP's default DNS servers are often slow. Switch to Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8). On Mac: System Settings → Wi-Fi → Details → DNS → click + → add 1.1.1.1. This often speeds up browsing noticeably.

6

Run Wireless Diagnostics on your Mac

Hold Option and click the Wi-Fi icon in the menu bar → Open Wireless Diagnostics. Follow the prompts — it scans your environment for channel conflicts, interference, and signal issues. It can also capture a packet log for deeper diagnosis.

7

Router placement matters more than you think

Put your router in a central, elevated location — not stuffed in a cabinet or corner. Keep it away from microwaves, cordless phones, and baby monitors (they all interfere with 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi). For large homes, consider a mesh system like eero or Google Nest Wifi.

Lower Your Internet Bill Starting Today

💰 The Retention Department Trick — Works Almost Every Time

Internet companies give their customer retention department authority to offer discounts, promotions, and loyalty credits that regular customer service reps cannot. Most people never know this department exists.

How it works: Call your ISP's main number. When asked what you're calling about, say "I'm thinking about canceling my service." You'll be transferred to retention. Be polite but firm — you want a lower rate. They almost always have a deal they can offer.

Hi, I've been a customer for [X] years but I've been looking at [competitor] and they're offering [price]. I love the service but I really need to bring my bill down. Is there anything you can do to help me stay?

This works because losing you costs them $300–$500 to replace. A $20/month discount is nothing to them. Call every 12 months when your promo rate expires — they'll almost always renew it.

Tips & Hacks

Get Better Internet for Less

🔌

Own your modem — stop renting

Most ISPs charge $10–$15/month to rent a modem/router. Buy a compatible one on Amazon for $60–$100 and it pays for itself in 6 months. Check your ISP's approved modem list before buying.

📶

2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz — know the difference

5 GHz Wi-Fi is faster but shorter range. 2.4 GHz is slower but penetrates walls better. On your Mac/iPhone, try connecting to your router's 5 GHz network (usually labeled with "5G" or "_5") when you're close to the router.

🧪

Test speed at different times of day

ISPs get congested in the evenings (7–10pm). If your internet is always slow at night, that's a network capacity issue in your area — call and report it. Many ISPs will credit your bill if you document the problem.
